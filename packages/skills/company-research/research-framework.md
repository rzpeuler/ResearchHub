# Company Research Framework

This document defines a repeatable research method, not a prompt or an Agent
runtime. Each module has a core question, analysis method, judgment standard,
and Evidence requirement.

## Business Understanding

**Core question:** How does the company create and capture value?

**Analysis method:** Map products, services, customers, channels, segments,
revenue structure, operating dependencies, and the conversion from resources
to revenue and cash flow.

**Judgment standard:** The business model must be stated in specific,
observable terms. A slogan or management claim without operating evidence is
not a business conclusion.

**Required Evidence:** Business description, segment/product facts, revenue
structure, customer/channel facts, and material operating dependencies.

## Industry Position

**Core question:** Where does the company sit in its industry and value chain?

**Analysis method:** Define the relevant industry, map competitors and
substitutes, examine market structure, regulation, cyclicality, supply,
demand, and the company's observable position.

**Judgment standard:** Industry opportunity must be separated from company
capture. Position claims require competitor, market, or operating evidence.

**Required Evidence:** Market or industry structure, competitor context,
value-chain facts, policy or regulatory facts, and company position indicators.

## Competitive Advantage

**Core question:** What can the company do better or protect more effectively
than competitors?

**Analysis method:** Test cost, scale, technology, IP, brand, distribution,
network effects, switching costs, licenses, and ecosystem advantages against
observable outcomes and potential erosion mechanisms.

**Judgment standard:** An advantage is a hypothesis until evidence shows a
durable economic effect such as retention, pricing, cost, access, or returns.

**Required Evidence:** Market share, margin or cost evidence, customer or
channel evidence, retention/switching evidence, technology/IP evidence, and
competitor comparisons where available.

## Growth Drivers

**Core question:** Through which mechanism could the company grow or improve
its economics?

**Analysis method:** Separate historical results from forward-looking drivers;
map volume, price, mix, penetration, capacity, geography, products, channels,
and operating leverage to measurable outcomes.

**Judgment standard:** Each driver needs a mechanism, current evidence,
dependency, invalidation condition, and validation metric.

**Required Evidence:** Historical growth, capacity, orders/backlog,
penetration, product-cycle, channel, pricing, or operating-leverage facts.

## Financial Quality

**Core question:** How strong, persistent, and cash-supported are the reported
business economics?

**Analysis method:** Examine revenue composition and growth, profitability,
margins, cash conversion, balance-sheet strength, leverage, working capital,
capital intensity, and period consistency.

**Judgment standard:** Financial quality claims must state period, unit, source,
and limitations. Financial facts do not become a valuation or recommendation.

**Required Evidence:** Revenue growth, operating profit, net profit, operating
cash flow, assets, liabilities, financial periods, units, and source metadata.

## Capital Allocation

**Core question:** How does management deploy internally generated and external
capital?

**Analysis method:** Compare stated priorities with reported capex, R&D,
acquisitions, dividends, repurchases, debt, liquidity, and disposals.

**Judgment standard:** The analysis records consistency, tradeoffs, and risks;
it does not infer management quality from one transaction.

**Required Evidence:** Capital expenditure, R&D, M&A, dividends, repurchases,
debt, liquidity, related-party, and governance-relevant facts.

## Risk Analysis

**Core question:** What could invalidate the company's business, growth, or
financial-quality Thesis?

**Analysis method:** Identify industry, competitive, execution, regulatory,
technology, concentration, financial, governance, and capital-allocation
risks; connect each risk to an assumption and warning signal.

**Judgment standard:** Risks must be specific enough to monitor. Generic risk
language without an affected assumption or signal is incomplete.

**Required Evidence:** Risk disclosures, concentration facts, regulatory or
policy records, leverage/liquidity facts, governance information, competitor
actions, and operational warning signals.

## Synthesis rule

The seven modules produce one linked Evidence set. A Thesis may summarize the
company's business quality or remain explicitly qualified when evidence is
insufficient. A Prediction must state what future metric and period would
confirm or weaken the Thesis.
